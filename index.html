Teacher Guide

Purpose: Use a Jeopardy-style game as an alternative interactive activity for Grade 11 General Mathematics, focusing on sequences and series and their applications. The game is formative: it helps identify concepts that need reteaching.

Materials: this HTML file; computer/laptop/tablet and projector if available; paper and pencil; optional calculator and formula sheet. No account or internet connection is needed after this file is saved.

Setup: Open the file in a current browser. Select Play the Game. Use pairs or small groups, or let learners play individually. Agree on answer format and partial-credit rules before starting.

Learning sequence (about 60 minutes)

1. Entry / instructions — 5 min

Introduce the goal and game rules. Show how to open a tile, check an answer, and return to the board.

Teacher role: Clarify rules and ensure everyone can access the activity.

2. Explanation / modeling — 10 min

Review arithmetic and geometric sequences/series and the meaning of variables in formulas. Model one question and explain the steps aloud.

Teacher role: Think aloud, connect formulas to meaning, and address vocabulary.

3. Guided practice — 15 min

Groups select questions and explain their strategies. Invite students to justify why a formula fits.

Teacher role: Prompt with questions, offer hints, and monitor participation.

4. Independent practice — 15 min

Learners solve selected questions independently or in pairs, showing calculations on paper.

Teacher role: Observe reasoning and note misconceptions without immediately giving answers.

5. Feedback — 10 min

Review solutions, compare methods, and correct errors in identifying terms, common difference/ratio, and financial formulas.

Teacher role: Give specific feedback and ask learners to revise their reasoning.

6. Short check — 5 min

Ask learners to answer the exit ticket below without help, then collect responses.

Teacher role: Check mastery and decide who needs follow-up instruction.

Short check of learning (exit ticket)

  1. Find the 8th term of 4, 7, 10, 13, …
  2. Find the sum of the first 5 terms of 2, 4, 6, 8, …
  3. ₱5,000 is invested at 6% annual compound interest for 2 years. Find the accumulated value.
  4. Explain one clue that helps you decide whether to use a sequence or a series.
  5. Create a short real-life situation that could be represented by a sequence or series and identify the information needed to solve it.
Exit-ticket answer / feedback guide
  1. 25; use aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d.
  2. 30; add the first five terms, or use Sₙ = n/2(a₁ + aₙ).
  3. ₱5,000(1.06)² = ₱5,618.
  4. A sequence asks for ordered terms; a series asks for their sum. Accept equivalent explanations.
  5. Answers vary. Look for a plausible context, identified quantities, and a reasonable model.

UDL and access adaptations

  • Multiple ways to access content: read each question aloud, provide a printed copy, and explain unfamiliar financial terms.
  • Multiple ways to participate: allow individual, pair, or small-group play; allow extra thinking time and quiet response time.
  • Multiple ways to show learning: accept typed, handwritten, spoken, or step-by-step answers. Score mathematical reasoning consistently.
  • Visual and language support: provide a formula sheet, worked example, large-print copy, and key vocabulary list.
  • Limited internet/device access: this file works offline. If there is only one device, project the board and have groups answer on paper; if no device is available, use the question list and answer key as printed cards.
  • Different learning needs: use high contrast, avoid relying on color alone, permit calculators when appropriate, and offer additional time or fewer questions without changing the learning target.
With the right support and flexible options, every learner can participate, explain, and grow.
