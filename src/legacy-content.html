Start Here: How to Solve a Word Problem

Do not rush to choose a formula. Read the problem, identify what the question asks, and explain why your formula fits. You may write your work on paper, talk it through with a partner, or type notes below.

1. GIVENList the important information, numbers, units, rate, and time.
2. ASKEDState exactly what you need to find: one term, a total, accumulated value, or payment.
3. FORMULA / MODELChoose the formula that matches the situation. Explain your choice.
4. SUBSTITUTEReplace the variables with the given values and show calculations.
5. ANSWER + CHECKInclude units, interpret the result, and check if it is reasonable.

Formula Sheet

Use this as a learning support. In a class assessment, your teacher will tell you whether it may be used.

Arithmetic sequence: one term

aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d

Use when the amount changes by the same difference each period. Find one particular term, not the total.

Arithmetic series: total

Sₙ = n/2 [2a₁ + (n − 1)d]

Or Sₙ = n/2 (a₁ + aₙ). Use when adding the first n terms of an arithmetic sequence.

Geometric sequence: one term

aₙ = a₁rⁿ⁻¹

Use when each term is multiplied by the same ratio.

Geometric series: total

Sₙ = a₁(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1), r ≠ 1

Use to find the sum of the first n terms of a geometric sequence. If r = 1, then Sₙ = na₁.

Compound interest: accumulated value

A = P(1 + i)ⁿ

P = starting principal; i = interest rate per compounding period as a decimal; n = number of periods. Use for a single starting amount growing with compound interest.

Equal periodic loan payments

R = P i / [1 − (1 + i)⁻ⁿ]

For equal payments at the end of each period on a loan with a fixed periodic interest rate. P = loan principal; i = rate per payment period; n = total number of payments. This formula assumes a standard fully amortizing loan and excludes fees or other charges.

Remember: a sequence is a list of terms; a series is the sum of terms. A single investment growing over time is different from repeated deposits or loan payments.

Worked Example: Savings Plan

Problem: Ana saves ₱100 in week 1 and increases the amount she saves by ₱50 every week. How much does she save in week 4? How much does she save altogether in the first 4 weeks?

  1. Given: first weekly deposit a₁ = ₱100, common difference d = ₱50, number of weeks n = 4.
  2. Asked: the deposit in week 4, and the total deposited in 4 weeks.
  3. Choose: use the arithmetic sequence formula for the week-4 deposit; use the arithmetic series formula for the total.
  4. Substitute: a₄ = 100 + (4 − 1)(50) = ₱250.
  5. Total: deposits are ₱100, ₱150, ₱200, ₱250. S₄ = 100 + 150 + 200 + 250 = ₱700.
  6. Check: the deposits rise by ₱50 each week, and their sum is ₱700.

Worked Example: Periodic Loan Payment

Problem: A borrower takes a ₱12,000 loan at 1% interest per month and repays it with 12 equal payments at the end of each month. Find the monthly payment. Ignore fees.

  1. Given: P = ₱12,000, i = 0.01 per month, n = 12 payments.
  2. Asked: the equal monthly payment R.
  3. Choose: use the periodic loan-payment formula because the loan is repaid through equal payments each month.
  4. Substitute: R = 12000(0.01) / [1 − (1.01)⁻¹²].
  5. Calculate: R ≈ ₱1,066.19 per month.
  6. Check: 12 payments total about ₱12,794.28, more than the original loan because interest is included. Small differences may occur from rounding.

Teacher note: use the calculator for this example and explain that the interest rate and payment period must use the same time unit.

How to Play

  1. Choose a category and point value. Questions progress from easier identification to more complex applications.
  2. Before calculating, state the given information and what is being asked.
  3. Write your solution on paper, explain orally to your partner, or type your reasoning in the notes box.
  4. Use the hint if needed. Reveal the worked solution to review each step.
  5. The teacher awards full or partial credit based on the answer and reasoning.

Teacher Guide

Target learners: Grade 11 Senior High School learners taking General Mathematics.

Learning focus: Apply sequences and series to financial problems, including accumulated investment values and periodic loan payments.

Purpose: This is a guided practice and formative assessment resource, not a replacement for explicit teaching. It is designed around learner-reported difficulties with word problems, formula recall, and the need for step-by-step explanations.

Materials: This HTML file saved on a device; laptop/computer/tablet and projector if available; paper and pencil; optional calculator; printed formula sheet and question cards if needed. No internet connection is required after the file is saved.

Scaffolding and accessibility (UDL)

  • Multiple ways to access: read questions aloud, print questions, explain financial vocabulary, and point to the formula sheet.
  • Multiple ways to participate: individual, pair, or group play; extra thinking time; oral discussion; or written response.
  • Multiple ways to show learning: accept typed, spoken, or handwritten solutions. Score mathematical reasoning using the same criteria.
  • Language support: clarify words such as principal, interest rate, accumulated value, installment, monthly payment, total, and period.
  • Limited technology: project the board and let learners solve on paper; or print the questions and worked solutions as cards. This HTML works offline once saved.
  • Different needs: use large print, formula sheet, calculator where appropriate, fewer questions with the same learning target, and additional time.

Important teacher note

The periodic loan-payment formula in this resource assumes equal payments at the end of each period, a fixed rate per period, and a standard fully amortizing loan. It does not include fees, insurance, or changing rates. Explain these assumptions and check that the formula matches the curriculum and lesson scope before use.
